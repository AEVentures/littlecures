## Thread 2 — Why pediatric cancer is different (4 posts)

**1 / 4**
Pediatric cancer is not "adult cancer, but smaller."

Kids' tumors are driven by fusion genes and epigenetics — not the same mutations adult drugs target. That is why adult immunotherapies frequently fail in children.

**2 / 4**
The economics are worse than most people realize. Pediatric cancers are rare. The market is small. Big pharma won't fund the R&D.

But rare + small ≠ unimportant. It just means the incentives are broken.

**3 / 4**
The data is siloed. Every hospital holds its own tiny dataset. Nobody has enough cases of DIPG or DMG on their own to train a reliable model.

Federated learning fixes this. Train across hospitals without moving patient data.

**4 / 4**
So we built **LittleCures** to route around all of it — in the open. Code, roadmap, and decisions all public.

If you care about this: https://littlecures.org

## Thread 3 — Global inequality of survival (3 posts)

**1 / 3**
The same childhood cancer has very different outcomes depending on where a child is born.

High-income country: ~80% survival.
Many low-income countries: <20% for the same disease.

This is not a science gap. It is an access gap.

**2 / 3**
Open science is the fastest way to close it. Open trial matching. Open drug targets. Open licensing. Open training pipelines.

Everything we ship at LittleCures is open by default, so no one is locked out.

**3 / 3**
If you agree that a child's life should not depend on the country they were born in, help us build.

🔗 https://littlecures.org
🐙 https://github.com/AEVentures/littlecures
