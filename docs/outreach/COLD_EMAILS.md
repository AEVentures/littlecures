# Cold Email Templates

Short. Personal. One clear ask. No attachments in first email.

---

## To a pediatric oncologist / hospital data lead

**Subject:** A tool for pediatric trial matching — 20 minutes of your time?

Hi Dr. [Last name],

I read [specific paper / talk / article they authored — one line]. Thank you for that work.

I am building an open-source, non-profit project called LittleCures. Our first tool is an open pediatric trial matching engine — one that returns relevant trials in seconds and explains them in plain language to families.

Before we go further, I would like 20 minutes of your feedback on what is broken in the current trial-matching workflow at [their institution] and what would actually be useful to you and your patients.

No commitment beyond a call. Everything we build is MIT-licensed and public.

Site: https://littlecures.org
Repo: https://github.com/AEVentures/littlecures

Would next Tuesday or Thursday work?

Thank you,
[Your name]

## To a journalist / newsletter writer

**Subject:** Story idea — open-source push against pediatric cancer

Hi [First name],

I follow [their newsletter / show / column] closely — [specific compliment tied to their work].

I want to pitch a story angle for you.

**Pediatric cancer's economics gap:** children with the deadliest cancers (DIPG, DMG, high-risk neuroblastoma) are effectively left out of the drug pipeline because the market is small. Meanwhile, in low-income countries, survival for the same diseases is a quarter of what it is here.

I am building **LittleCures**, an open-source, non-profit response — federated learning across hospitals, an open trial-matching engine, and an open target registry. Everything public, everything MIT-licensed.

If this feels like a fit, I would welcome a 20-minute conversation.
