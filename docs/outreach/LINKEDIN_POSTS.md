## Post 1 — Announcement (LinkedIn-native)

I have been quietly working on something that I want to share.

Pediatric cancer research is underfunded, siloed, and unequal. Most cancer drugs are built for adults. Children with the deadliest pediatric cancers — like DIPG and DMG — have seen almost no survival improvement in decades. In many low-income countries, survival for the same disease is a quarter of what it is in the U.S. or Europe.

The problem is not that we lack science. It is that the incentives, the data, and the access are broken.

So we started **LittleCures** — an open-source, patient-first project to build the shared infrastructure the field needs:

▸ Open trial matching for pediatric cancers
▸ An open registry of molecular drivers, active trials, and patent status
▸ Federated learning software so hospitals can train shared models on rare tumor data without moving patient data
▸ Access and advocacy to keep discoveries affordable and available

It is open source. It is non-profit. It is patient-first. And it is under the AEVentures organization on GitHub.

If you are an engineer, researcher, clinician, designer, or advocate, there is a place for you.

🌐 https://littlecures.org
🐙 https://github.com/AEVentures/littlecures
📄 White paper: https://github.com/AEVentures/littlecures/blob/main/docs/WHITEPAPER.md

I would love your help. Star the repo. Share this post. Introduce me to someone I should meet.

Every child, every cure.

## Post 2 — For clinicians and hospitals

Pediatric oncologists and hospital data teams: I want to hear from you.

We are building **LittleCures**, an open-source infrastructure project focused on childhood cancer. The first tool we are shipping is an open trial matching engine that helps clinicians and families find relevant pediatric trials in seconds — with plain-language explanations that a parent can understand.

Before we build the wrong thing, we need feedback from the people who would actually use it:

- What is broken in your current workflow for trial matching?
- Where do families get stuck when they ask you about experimental options?
- Would your hospital pilot an open, federated system for shared model training?

If you are willing to have a 20-minute conversation, please DM me or open a discussion on GitHub.

🌐 https://littlecures.org
🐙 https://github.com/AEVentures/littlecures/discussions

## Post 3 — For funders

To foundations, family offices, and donor-advised funds:

**LittleCures** is an open-source project accelerating cures for children with cancer, focused on the deadliest and most neglected diseases.

Unlike a traditional biotech, everything we build is public and free to use. That means:

▸ Every dollar of philanthropic capital funds shared infrastructure, not proprietary IP.
▸ The tools we build are usable by any hospital or research group, anywhere.
▸ Our roadmap and metrics live on GitHub, in public.

We are looking for founding funders who care about pediatric oncology, open science, or access to care in low- and middle-income countries.

If this resonates, I would welcome a conversation.
