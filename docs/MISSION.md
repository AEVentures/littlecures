# LittleCures Mission

## The problem

Cancer is the leading cause of disease-related death in children in high-income countries. In low- and middle-income countries, the majority of children with cancer never receive a diagnosis or abandon treatment.

Some pediatric cancers are now highly curable. Acute lymphoblastic leukemia, the most common childhood cancer, reaches roughly 90% long-term survival in wealthy nations. But that success is not shared globally. And for other diseases — especially certain brain tumors like DIPG/DMG, relapsed neuroblastoma, and metastatic sarcomas — survival has barely changed in decades.

The incentives of drug development are not aligned with children. Pharmaceutical companies target large adult markets. Pediatric cancers are rare, fragmented into dozens of subtypes, and biologically different from adult cancers. Most drugs used in children are adult drugs given off-label, often at toxic doses.

Data is siloed. Each children's hospital has only a handful of cases for any given rare tumor. No single institution has enough data to train reliable predictive models, yet institutions cannot easily share patient data across borders.

## Our position

LittleCures exists to build **open, public infrastructure** for pediatric oncology. We believe:

- A child’s chance of survival should not depend on where they are born or how large their disease’s market is.
- The most neglected pediatric cancers deserve modern tools and coordinated effort.
- Open source, open data, and open science can de-risk and accelerate work that private markets undervalue.
- Patient data must be protected, but scientific models, code, and knowledge should flow freely.

## What we are building

We are starting with four focus areas. Each is chosen because it is both high-leverage and feasible for a distributed, volunteer-driven team.

### 1. Open pediatric trial matching

Most pediatric cancer patients are eligible for clinical trials, but fewer than one in ten are matched to one. Oncologists and families lack a fast, unified way to search across COG, institutional, national, and international trial registries. We will build a public, open-source tool that parses eligibility criteria and ranks trials against a child's profile.

### 2. Open target and IP registry

There is no single public map of the molecular drivers, patent landscape, active trials, cell lines, PDX models, and sequencing datasets for the most lethal pediatric cancers. We will build one. This registry will help researchers avoid duplication, identify gaps, and understand what intellectual property stands between a target and a child.

### 3. Federated learning for rare tumors

We will develop or integrate federated learning tools that allow hospitals to train shared models on rare tumor data without centralizing patient data. This addresses the central statistical problem of pediatric oncology: the cases are everywhere, but never in one place.

### 4. Access and advocacy infrastructure

We will support open intellectual property, equitable licensing, and policy tools that keep discoveries affordable and globally available. This includes advocacy around the RACE for Children Act and the rare pediatric disease priority review voucher program.

## Who we need

- Software engineers and ML engineers
- Designers and technical writers
- Pediatric oncologists, pathologists, and researchers
- Bioinformaticians and data scientists
- Patient advocates, ethicists, and policy specialists
- Anyone who believes this work should not wait

## Principles

- **Patient-first.** Every decision is judged by its effect on children and families.
- **Open by default.** Code, documentation, and non-sensitive data are public unless there is a compelling reason otherwise.
- **Privacy-preserving.** We do not centralize identifiable patient data.
- **Global.** We design for access in low-resource settings from the beginning.
- **Honest.** We do not overstate results, promise timelines we cannot keep, or treat children as marketing.

## What success looks like

In five years, we want:

- A clinician anywhere in the world to find the best available trial for a child with a rare cancer in under one minute.
- A researcher to see, within minutes, who owns the relevant IP for a pediatric target and what models are available.
- Multiple institutions to train shared models on rare pediatric cancers without sharing raw patient data.
- New, open tools that no single institution would have built alone.

We are not a hospital, a pharmaceutical company, or a charity. We are a public commons. If this speaks to you, join us.
