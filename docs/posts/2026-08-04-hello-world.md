# Hello, world.

*Published August 4, 2026 · The first post from LittleCures.*

---

Today we are opening the doors on **LittleCures** — an open-source, patient-first project to accelerate cures for children with cancer.

We are not opening a company. We are not opening a clinic. We are opening a public workshop, and asking the internet to help us build it.

## Why this exists

Pediatric cancer is scientifically hard, economically unrewarded, and geographically unequal.

- Most cancer drugs are developed for adults. Children with the deadliest pediatric cancers — DIPG, DMG, high-risk neuroblastoma — have seen almost no survival improvement in decades.
- Every hospital holds a small, siloed dataset. For rare tumors, no single institution has enough cases on its own to train a reliable model.
- In high-income countries, roughly four out of five children with cancer survive. In many low-income countries, four out of five die from the same diseases.

The problem is not that we lack science. It is that the incentives, the data, and the access are broken. We can route around all three.

## What we are going to build, in the open

Four pillars:

1. **Open trial matching.** A public tool that returns relevant pediatric trials in seconds, in plain language a family can understand.
2. **An open target registry.** A clear map of the molecular drivers, active trials, and patent status of the most neglected childhood cancers.
3. **Federated learning across hospitals.** Software that lets hospitals train shared models on rare tumor data without moving patient records outside their walls.
4. **Access and advocacy.** Open IP and equitable licensing so what we build stays affordable and available.

Everything is MIT-licensed. Data schemas are public. The roadmap lives on GitHub. Decisions are reviewed in public.

## Why open source, and why now

Because the alternative is worse. Traditional biotech takes small markets and either ignores them or extracts monopoly rents from the families who can least afford it. A patent-encumbered, proprietary approach to pediatric oncology fails on both moral and practical grounds.

Open source has quietly become the most reliable way to build shared infrastructure — Linux, PostgreSQL, the modern web, most of applied AI. There is no reason pediatric cancer infrastructure should be an exception.

## What we need from you

- **If you are an engineer:** star the repo, pick a `good first issue`, ship a PR.
- **If you are a researcher or clinician:** open a discussion. Tell us what tool would actually change your day. Review our clinical assumptions.
- **If you are a designer or writer:** help us make this legible to families and policymakers, not just scientists.
- **If you are an advocate or funder:** share the site, introduce us to your network, sponsor the work or an individual contributor.
- **If you are a family that has lived this:** open a discussion and tell us what you have learned. You are why this exists, and we will build for you first.

## The next 90 days

- Ship an MVP of open trial matching.
- Publish the first version of the open target registry, starting with DIPG/DMG.
- Onboard the first ten technical contributors.
- Open conversations with the first three hospitals and the first three funders.

We will post updates weekly, publicly, honestly — including when things do not work.
